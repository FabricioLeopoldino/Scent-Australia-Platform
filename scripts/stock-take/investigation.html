Where the Fragrance Went

The count of 28 August found the system carrying 830 litres of oil that was not on the shelf. This is what the records say about how that happened.

The question

Was this the system getting it wrong?

No. Every movement the platform was told about, it recorded correctly — the arithmetic closes to the millilitre in the one place it can be checked exactly. The shortfall sits almost entirely on the eighteen oils the technicians handled, and it runs one way only. The oil left without anybody entering that it had left.

Four things were tested

Each one could have shown a fault in the platform. None did.

1 · Did stock ever change without a movement being recorded?

3,196 of 3,454 balance changes have a movement behind them.

The database keeps a second, independent record of every change to a stock figure, written automatically and read by nothing. Comparing it against the movement history for the three months to the count: 180 of the 258 unmatched changes happened on one day, 8 July — the migration, across 170 oils, which is what setting opening balances looks like and is expected. Almost all of the rest are tiny same-day edit-and-undo pairs of a tenth of a litre. Since the platform went live, it is not losing movements.

2 · Does the movement history add up?

1,764 of 1,812 steps are arithmetically consistent — 97.4%.

Each movement records the balance it left behind, so every step can be checked against the quantity beside it. The 48 that disagree come to roughly 120 litres in total, and 25 of them are stock entries differing by 2.5 litres between them. There is no systematic error here — no movement type that deducts twice, none that fails to deduct.

All 28 transfers out of main stock match their corresponding entry into technician stock, with no exceptions. This is the important one. The system's own books were perfect. The oil was not there.

4 · Where does the shortfall actually sit?

On the eighteen oils the technicians handled.

They are 11% of the fragrances counted and 57% of the total shortfall. The obvious objection is that those are simply the busiest oils, so comparing them only against quiet ones would prove nothing. So they are compared against oils with the same level of activity:

−26.4 L 18 oils the technicians handled
average shortfall each · 110 movements each
every single one short · −474.9 L in total
±9.2 L 39 oils, same activity, no technicians
average error each · 46 movements each
over as often as under · −75.8 L in total

The two look nothing alike. The oils without technicians are wrong by about nine litres each, and those errors point in both directions — they cancel out to under two litres. That is ordinary measurement noise: what clings to a drum, what gets rounded, what spills. The technician oils are wrong by nearly three times as much, and every one of them is short. Nothing came out over.

An error that only ever goes one way is not noise. It is something leaving and not being written down.

What that means happened

Keeping fragrance in two places asked the warehouse to record a movement every time oil crossed between them: taken out to a technician, used on a job, brought back, found and returned. Each of those was a separate entry somebody had to remember, on top of the work itself.

When one is missed, nothing complains. The system carries on with figures that agree with each other perfectly — as they did, to the millilitre — while the shelf quietly drifts away from them. It stays invisible until somebody counts.

The system did contribute one thing. Not a fault, a design decision: it offered a second place to keep fragrance, and a second place is a second set of entries to get right. That is exactly why the technician stock was retired on 31 August and fragrance went back to one number per oil. Fewer entries to miss.

What this cannot tell you

  • How much came across already wrong. On 8 July, 170 opening balances were loaded from the previous system. Nothing verified them, and there is no earlier history here to compare against, so some part of the gap may predate the platform entirely. The correlation with technician handling argues that most of it accrued afterwards, but it cannot separate the two completely.
  • Whether any single entry was missed on purpose. This only shows that entries are missing, not why. The pattern — spread across eighteen oils and five months — reads as routine steps being skipped under time pressure, not as one event.
  • Two oils are excluded. Milagrito and Myrrh & Tonka were held back from the adjustment on the warehouse's word and are not in these figures.

What would stop it happening again

  • Already done: one number per oil, so there is no second ledger to keep in step. Every technician movement now records who made it, which it did not before — 386 movements across five months read only as “System”.
  • Count the eighteen again in a month. If the shortfall stops growing, retiring the second ledger fixed it. If it keeps growing, the loss is somewhere else and this report was wrong about the cause. That is the test worth running.
  • The daily check now watches for a new negative balance in both businesses, which it did not before 25 August — it was only looking at half the warehouse.
Every figure here is read from the platform's own records: the movement history, and the separate automatic log of stock changes that nothing else reads. Period examined 30 May to 28 August 2026, fragrance oils only. Nothing in this report is an estimate.
